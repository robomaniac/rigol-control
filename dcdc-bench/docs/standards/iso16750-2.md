# ISO 16750-2:2023, section 4 (electrical loads): clause map and bench verdicts

This page lists every clause of section 4 of ISO 16750-2:2023 with its test
parameters for 12 V and 24 V systems, the instrument capability each clause
needs, and what that means on the project's bench (Rigol DP821A CH1 as the
source, 0-35.8 V / 1 A in the purpose-limited profile, commanded over LAN at
about 1 s; Rigol DL3031A as the load). The machine-readable form is
`src/dcdc_bench/standards.py`; the bench page's "Which test?" checklist reads
that module, not this page.

Only technical facts (clause numbers, titles, levels, durations, rates,
impedances, functional status classes) are recorded here, cited as
"ISO 16750-2:2023, clause 4.x". The standard's text is not reproduced and the
reference copy is not stored in the repository (implementation brief §16).
Values that appear only inside a figure of the standard are marked
**figure value**; confirm them on the printed copy before relying on them.

## Definitions used below

- **UN** nominal system voltage: 12 V or 24 V. **UA** supply voltage with the
  alternator running: 14 V / 28 V. **UB** with the alternator stopped:
  12 V / 24 V. (ISO 16750-1.)
- **Usmin / Usmax** minimum and maximum supply voltage of the chosen code
  (ISO 16750-2:2023, clause 4.2, Tables 3 and 4):

  | System | Code | Usmin | Usmax |
  | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
  | 12 V | A | 6 V | 16 V |
  | 12 V | B | 8 V | 16 V |
  | 12 V | C | 9 V | 16 V |
  | 12 V | D | 10.5 V | 16 V |
  | 24 V | E | 10 V | 32 V |
  | 24 V | F | 16 V | 32 V |
  | 24 V | H | 18 V | 32 V |
  | 24 V | G | 22 V | 32 V |
  | both | Z | as agreed | as agreed |

  For the 12T12-4A (stated 9-36 V input) the module selects the code whose
  range fits inside the DUT rating with the lowest Usmin: **C** for 12 V,
  **E** for 24 V.
- **Operating modes** (ISO 16750-1): 1.x not energised; 2.x energised, functions
  not activated (2.1 sleep); 3.2 normal operating load; 3.3 minimum load;
  3.4 maximum load.
- **Functional status classes** (ISO 16750-1), one line each:
  **A** every function works as designed during and after the test;
  **B** every function works during the test, some may leave tolerance, all
  recover by themselves afterwards;
  **C** some functions stop during the test and come back by themselves
  afterwards;
  **D** some functions stop during the test and need a simple operator reset;
  **E** some functions stop and do not come back without repair or replacement.
- **General tolerances** (clause 4.1): frequency and time +/-5 %, voltage
  +/-0.2 V, current +/-2 %, inductance +/-10 %, resistance +/-10 %. Voltages
  are measured at the DUT terminals; at least two DUTs are used.

## Clause table

Capability tokens are defined in the module docstring of `standards.py`.
"Verdict" is `feasibility()` for the 12T12-4A on the seeded real bench profile
(source 35.8 V / 1 A, approved DUT-input overvoltage guard 26 V). The mock
bench earns the same tokens (the same instrument class) but not the same
verdicts: every procedure other than the 4.2 DC level subset exists on the
synthetic plant only, so 4.3.1.1, 4.5 and 4.6.2 read **mock only** on a real
profile and **runs here after approval** on the simulated bench, and the
best-effort clauses (4.3.1.2, 4.3.2, 4.6.1.1, 4.6.1.2, 4.9.1, 4.9.2;
[best-effort-proposal.md](best-effort-proposal.md), owner decisions of
2026-10-01) read **mock only** on a real profile and **best effort
(deviations recorded)** on the simulated bench. The 26 V guard condition on
4.2 at 24 V and on the 26 V jump start exists on the real profile only
because `profiles/bench/mock.yaml` declares no approved guard. The verdict
"needs split" is no longer produced: a long recipe (4.3.1.1, 4.5) declares its own
`authorization.instrument_timed_bound_s` and is approved with it (owner
decision 1). The section [Which clauses run where](#which-clauses-run-where)
lists this per clause.

A best-effort verdict carries a **deviation sheet**: one row per parameter
the clause records, with the clause value and tolerance (clause-specific or
the clause 4.1 general tolerances), the bench value and its basis (DP800
datasheet p. 5 slew and programming accuracy, the measured 4-55 ms LAN round
trips plus the < 118 ms command processing, the supply's whole-second
Timer/Delayer), the mechanism, whether the bench measures it, and one of four
classifications by a mechanical rule: `met` (bound inside the tolerance),
`approximated` (a different kind of mechanism, not entirely outside),
`not_met_but_documented` (bound entirely outside), `unknown_until_measured`
(no bound, or straddling). Terminal-side timings stay "commanded, not
measured" until a scope or DAQ exists; the word "achieved" is reserved for
measured values. The generated recipe carries the sheet and is approved with
its hash (`authorization.best_effort_approved`,
`authorization.accepted_deviations_sha256`); an edited sheet invalidates the
approval. A level equal to the converter's stated maximum (36 V at 24 V for
4.3.1.1 and 4.3.2) is programmed at the project's 35.8 V margin setting and
recorded as approximated unless the recipe declares
`authorization.program_clause_level_exactly` (owner decision 6).

## Numbering versus the owner's recollection

The owner remembered 4.6.2 for reset behaviour, 4.6.4 for load dump and 4.7
for reversed voltage. All three match the 2023 numbering used in this page and
in `standards.py`. The 2023 edition splits 4.3 into 4.3.1.1 (alternator
regulator failure), 4.3.1.2 (jump start) and 4.3.2 (transient overvoltage),
and 4.6.1 into 4.6.1.1 (momentary drop) and 4.6.1.2 (micro interruption).
Which of these sub-clauses are new tests and which are earlier tests given
their own number is **to be verified against the owner's copy** of the
standard: the repository holds no earlier edition, and the power-electronics
review recalls jump start and transient overvoltage as existing tests of the
2012 edition, so this page asserts no edition history. Pulses 4 and 5 of
older ISO 7637-2 editions are the starting profile (4.6.3) and load dump
(4.6.4) here.

`authorization.uvlo_approved: true` and `authorization.protective_policy_id`

declared in that bench profile. Until then every level below the converter's
9 V minimum plans as `approval_blocked`. The shipped `mock.yaml` declares no
protective block, so approving a recipe against it also means declaring one.

Approval of a best-effort recipe (and of the 4.3.1.1 hold, which uses the
same test type) is the same gate extended (owner decision 4):
`authorization.best_effort_approved: true`,
`authorization.accepted_deviations_sha256` equal to the hash of the recipe's

test it is the sheet's own `sha256()`), the protective policy id, and
`dut_input_overvoltage_V` declared in the bench profile as well; a recipe
longer than the 720 s source timer also needs its declared
`authorization.instrument_timed_bound_s` (4.3.1.1: 3700 s). Wherever the
input goes below the converter's minimum (the 4.5 V drop, every output-OFF
interruption) `authorization.uvlo_approved` is needed as well.
`planning.best_effort_approval_gaps` lists whatever is missing; the planner
refuses (never clips) a level above the converter's maximum, a 36 V level
without `program_clause_level_exactly`, and a phase longer than the bound in
force.

The §4.2 badge says **DC level subset** and its coverage is `partial`. The
generated recipe measures separate cold-started DC levels; it does not run
the t1/t2 hold profile or 1 V/s transitions. The standards parameter table
below describes the referenced procedure, not an implemented waveform. The
recipe scope is retained in the HTML/PDF method section.

## The numbers a recipe needs

`recipe_parameters()` derives these from the standard and the DUT rating
(12T12-4A, 9-36 V). The 4.2 recipe is an ordinary `steady_state_load_sweep`;
the 4.5 and 4.6.2 recipes are executed by `supply_profiles.py` on the
synthetic plant only (the real bench refuses their test types at planning);
4.3.1.1, 4.3.1.2 and 4.3.2 generate `transient_hold`, 4.6.1.1
`momentary_drop`, 4.6.1.2 `micro_interruption` and 4.9.1 / 4.9.2
`line_interruption` recipes, each with its deviation sheet
(`deviation_sheet()`), on the synthetic plant only. The general real recipe
runner is still disabled and the below-minimum steps need the approved
UVLO-style recipe path (brief §7.5). The real path bounds a run by a 540 s
planning estimate, a 660 s software deadline and a 720 s one-shot source timer
per input-voltage phase; the "longest stretch" `feasibility()` compares with
that envelope becomes the recipe's declared `instrument_timed_bound_s` where
it is longer. Every best-effort recipe suggests a source OVP just above its
top level (14 V minimum; 20 V for 18 V, 28 V for 26 V, 38 V for 36 V) and
runs at a fixed 0.1 A load.
